1. Host sets up an event

Who the person throwing the partyWhere phone or laptopTime ten minutes with an uploaded invite, less for text only

  1. Lands on the site

    One line of pitch, one button: Continue with Google. First sign-in creates their profile from their Google name and email. Nothing else to fill in.

  2. Dashboard

    First visit shows a single card, Create your first event. Later visits list events with date, people coming and no-reply counts, plus a New event button.

  3. Basics

    Event type first (kids' party, adult birthday, baby shower, engagement, housewarming or farewell, family gathering, community or school, workshop), which sets sensible defaults for every later switch. Then title, the date picked from a calendar, start time from a time picker with an optional end time (and an optional free-text line for things like "till late"), venue, address with an Open in Google Maps option (Apple Maps on iPhone) generated from it automatically, RSVP-by date, the host line ("Leo's mum and dad") and a phone for questions.

  4. Details

    Intro, good to know, what we'll serve (optional: pick from a list such as BBQ, lunch, dinner, brunch, morning tea, afternoon tea and cake, drinks and nibbles, pizza, high tea, or write your own; this same line heads the bring-a-plate list), what to bring ("swimmers, towel, rashie"), gifts as two independent settings: a stance (No gifts, Optional, Wish list link, Books only, plus a note) a Group gift switch that can sit alongside any of them, and optional gift preference chips in two rows, "happy with" (second-hand, hand-me-downs, homemade, books, experiences, vouchers) and "please avoid" (batteries, plastic, noisy toys, lollies, anything large), which the invite turns into one friendly sentence, The place, a small group under the address: parking (free text), facilities as tap-to-pick chips (showers, change rooms, toilets, shade, pool fence, playground, BBQ, kitchen, wheelchair access, pram friendly) plus a free line, public transport, accessibility at the venue, and getting in (gate code, buzzer, which door, shown only to guests who said yes), and for kids' parties two controls: a three-way choice (Parents welcome to stay, Drop-off party, Either) and a Siblings welcome checkbox. A Save the date switch lives here too.

  5. Look

    Three cards side by side: Text only, Choose a theme (a gallery of six), Upload my invite (PDF, PNG or JPG). A live phone preview sits beside them and updates as they click. Accent colour is picked from the image or theme with an override. Upload mode has a switch for the small details strip under the picture, on by default. Below: the share card preview, showing how the link will look in iMessage and WhatsApp, with editable title and description.

  6. Ask guests

    What the RSVP collects: party size as one number or children and adults separately; names of everyone coming; dietary needs with an editable chip list; accessibility needs; one custom question; bring-a-plate on or off; group gift on or off, with the organiser set now or later.

  7. Guests

    Four ways in: add one (name, mobile, contact name for a child), paste a list one per line, pick from the phone's contacts, or copy from a past event. Guests can be tagged into groups the host names themselves (for example school, family, Leo's soccer team; typed once, then offered as chips, renamable at any time) and given roles (speaker, helper). Every guest gets their personal link the moment they are added.

  8. Share

    The text template with {name} and {link}, editable. Per guest: Text, WhatsApp, Share, Copy. A Send next mode walks the list with a counter. The group link with Copy and Share buttons and an on/off switch, plus a downloadable QR code meant only for print: a paper invite, a school noticeboard, a sign at the venue. Phone to phone, the host shares the link itself. Add a co-host by email or link.

  9. Done

    Lands on the event dashboard, journey 2, with Add to Google Calendar and Apple / Outlook calendar buttons for the host's own diary, the same ones guests get. Everything above can be edited at any time; guest links never change.

For design

  • The Look step is the signature screen: three cards plus a live phone preview, and it must work on a phone as well as a laptop.
  • Basics and Details are one long form on mobile with sticky Save. On desktop the preview stays visible throughout.
  • Event type should feel like the first friendly choice, not a settings dropdown.

2. Host and co-host, before the day

Who everyone organisingWhere mostly phoneTime a glance a day, a few minutes for reminders

  1. Co-host joins

    Gets a text or email from the host with a link, signs in with Google, and the event is on their dashboard. Same powers as the host except deleting the event and removing co-hosts, and the same calendar buttons for their own diary. Every action shows who did it.

  2. Event dashboard

    Counts first: people coming (children and adults if split), said yes, said no, no reply, opened but not replied. Under them a summary line of dietary and accessibility needs, and a nudge strip: "RSVP closes in 3 days, 6 people haven't replied."

  3. Guest list

    Filter by status, group or role. Each row: name and contact, reply with numbers, a trail of sent, opened, replied, who added and who texted them, dietary chips, their plate item, gift tick. Actions: Text, WhatsApp, Copy, View, New link, Merge, Remove.

  4. Remind

    Opens the no-reply list with opened-but-not-replied first. Separate reminder template. Same buttons, same Send next flow. Each guest shows when they were last reminded.

  5. Post an update

    "Rain plan: we're moving indoors." The update appears at the top of every guest's invite, and one tap texts a nudge to everyone who said yes.

  6. Bring-a-plate board

    The host's "what we'll serve" line and the mode (optional, or everyone brings), then everything guests have claimed or added, who is bringing it, dish tags, anything the host asked for still unclaimed. The allergy banner as guests see it, plus the host's own line above it. Host can edit or remove any item.

  7. Group gift

    Set the gift and target, choose the organiser (me or a guest), text them the handover. Status shows once the organiser has added their details, and who has chipped in unless the surprise switch hides it.

  8. Runsheet

    Items with time, what happens, who is on it, and visibility: hosts only, all guests, or specific guests or roles. Reorder by drag. Guest-visible items appear on the invite as "On the day".

  9. Closing the list

    Switch the group link off, or end save-the-date mode to reveal the full invite. Each has a one-tap nudge to tell guests. There is no guest cap: hosts invite the right number.

For design

  • The dashboard is operated, not read: counts and the nudge strip above the fold, the list below.
  • The sent, opened, replied trail needs a compact visual per row that survives a 390px screen.
  • Send next is a focused one-guest-at-a-time screen with a progress count, not the full table.

3. Guest with a personal link

Who anyone the host texted directlyWhere their phone, in the browserTime under a minute to reply

In their chat

Hi Sarah! You're invited to Leo's 6th. See the details and RSVP: link

Then the preview card: the invite image, "You're invited: Leo's 6th Birthday", the app's domain.

  1. Taps the link

    The invite opens: "Hi Oliver, you're invited", then the uploaded invite full width (tap to zoom), or the theme, or the text layout. Any update the host posted sits at the top.

  2. Reads the details

    When and where with an Open in Google Maps button (Apple Maps on iPhone), kept to date, time and address only. Then the detail rows as icon lines: what we'll serve, what to bring, parking, facilities and public transport (getting-in details appear only after a yes), good to know, what to bring, gifts, accessibility at the venue, "On the day" timeline if the host shared one, whether parents stay or drop off and whether siblings are welcome, and "Questions? Text Leo's mum".

  3. Yes or no

    Two big buttons. The wording can be customised per event ("I choose you!").

  4. If yes, a few quick questions

    Only the ones the host switched on, in this order: how many (one stepper, or children and adults), names of everyone coming, dietary chips and an allergy line, accessibility chips, the custom question, a note for the host. Emergency contact if it is a drop-off party. When siblings are welcome, the children count and names include them, so no separate question is needed. Everything optional except the count.

  5. Thank-you screen

    What they told us, repeated back. Add to Google Calendar and Apple / Outlook calendar buttons. Then the extras, each its own card: bring-a-plate (claim something or add their own, allergy banner at the top), group gift (who is organising, how to chip in, "I've chipped in"). And Change my answer.

  6. If no

    "Sorry you'll miss it", an optional note, done. The link still works if plans change.

  7. Coming back later

    The same link shows their reply, any updates, the plate list and the gift, and on the day the live timeline. Nothing to log into.

  8. Wrong person

    A small "Not Oliver? Reply here instead" link at the bottom sends them into journey 4.

For design

  • This page is the product. Its structure is the illustrated strip (cover, the details, the day, good to know, RSVP, after), decided in design round one. Three looks (text, theme, upload) share that layout below the cover. The theme gallery is illustration sets in mixed styles, one-ink and full colour; the one-ink look is an option, not the default.
  • The questions after yes should feel like one short conversation, not a form. Chips over dropdowns everywhere.
  • The thank-you screen carries the extras, so it needs a clear hierarchy: confirmation, calendar, then the plate and gift cards.

4. Guest from the group link

Who anyone who found the link in a group chat, on a post or via a QR codeWhere their phone

  1. Taps the link

    Same invite and preview card. The greeting reads "You're invited to Leo's 6th! Tell us whether you can make it."

  2. Yes or no, then who they are

    The same two big buttons first. The first question after is "Who's this from?": name (child's name and a contact name for a kids' party), mobile optional "in case the host needs to reach you". Then the same questions as journey 3.

  3. Gets their own link

    After sending, they land on a personal link of their own, with "Save this link to change your answer later" and a Text me my link button that opens Messages to themselves.

  4. On the host's side

    The reply appears tagged "via group link". If the name or number matches someone already invited who had not replied, it attaches to them instead of creating a second row. The occasional duplicate is a one-tap merge.

  5. When the link is closed

    "RSVPs have closed" with a text-the-host button, so a late finder is not left guessing.

5. Save the date, both sides

Who hosts sending early, guests receiving early

  1. Host switches on save-the-date mode

    Needs only a title and a date. The look can be text, theme, or an uploaded save-the-date image. Guests are added and texted exactly as in journey 1.

  2. Guest opens the link

    Title, date, "details coming soon", and one button: Got it, I'll keep the date free. Optional calendar hold. Because the link is theirs, the tap is recorded against their name (group-link guests are asked "Who's this from?" first).

  3. Host sees who has seen it

    The guest list shows each person's status by name: not opened, opened, or keeping the date free with the time they tapped. Counts sit above, and the usual reminder buttons cover those who have not responded.

  4. Host completes the event

    Fills in the details, swaps the image for the full invite, switches the mode off. One tap texts "details are up" to everyone.

  5. Guest opens the same link again

    Full invite and the yes and no buttons. Nothing new to receive or find.

6. Special guest with a role

Who a speaker, MC, performer, photographer or helper

  1. Host gives a guest a role

    On the guest row. Runsheet items can then be assigned to that person or to the role.

  2. Their invite grows a "Your part" panel

    Above the general timeline: their items with times, who to find when they arrive, and a text-the-host button. They still RSVP like everyone else.

  3. The night before

    Host filters the list by role and texts a reminder with the usual buttons.

7. Group gift organiser

Who a guest (or the host) running the group presentMoney never passes through the app in phase 1

  1. Host sets up the gift

    What it is, an optional target, and the organiser picked from the guest list or "me".

  2. Organiser gets the handover text

    "Would you organise the group gift for Gabe? Everything is here: link". Their personal link opens an organiser page.

  3. Organiser adds their details and a message for contributors

    PayID, bank details or a gift-fund link, plus everything a guest needs to chip in: a suggested amount, a chip-in-by date, what to write in the payment reference, and a note ("We're getting the blue scooter from Anaconda, $20 each is plenty"). The organiser can edit this any time and post short updates to contributors ("Target reached, buying Saturday"). A surprise switch hides contributions from the hosts when the gift is for one of them.

  4. Guests see the gift block

    On the thank-you screen and on their link: "Sarah is organising a group gift, a scooter", the organiser's message with the suggested amount and date, how to chip in, the latest update if there is one, and an I've chipped in tick with an optional amount.

  5. Organiser keeps track

    Who has chipped in, the running total from entered amounts, Text or WhatsApp nudges for those who have not, and a text to everyone who chipped in when the gift is bought.

8. On the day

Who hosts, co-hosts, guests, special guests

  1. The day before: "See you soon"

    The dashboard nudges the host to send it. One template, prefilled with the address, the Open in Maps link, the host's access info (gate code, buzzer, where to park, which door) and the start time. Same Text, WhatsApp and Send next buttons, to everyone who said yes only. Access info is a separate field on the event, shown only to guests who said yes, never on the group link.

  2. Guest gets the message

    "See you tomorrow! 12 Example St, gate code 4321, park on the street. Everything you need: link". Their link now opens on a see-you-soon view: address and access info first, the day's timeline, and one button, Sorry, I can no longer make it, which flips their reply to no and lets them leave a note. Late drop-outs show on the dashboard with a time, so the host can adjust catering.

  3. Hosts open the event

    The dashboard leads with the runsheet in a live "now / next" view, host-only items included, and the final numbers underneath, including any late drop-outs.

  4. Guests open their link

    The public timeline in the same now / next view: "2:30 cake, 3:30 pick-up". Special guests see their own items highlighted on top.

  5. Something changes

    Post an update, nudge everyone who said yes. Same buttons as before.

9. After the party

Who host sending thanks, guests receiving them

  1. Event switches to "Say thanks"

    The day after, the dashboard offers it. The list narrows to who came. Host uploads a photo per guest (Gabe opening their present) and a few shared photos for everyone.

  2. Sends

    Thank-you template, Text or WhatsApp per guest, Send next. Guests without a personal photo get the shared version. Sent and opened tracked as before.

  3. Guest taps the link

    Their thank-you page: the message, their photo full size with tap to save, then the shared photos.

10. Next time

Who a host who runs events more than once

  1. Duplicate event

    Copies details, look, settings, plate items, runsheet and templates. Date and RSVP-by cleared. Guest list optional.

  2. Copy a guest list

    From any past event, all or by group. Fresh links for everyone; replies, dietary notes and photos are not carried over, groups and roles are.

  3. Delete an event

    Wipes guests, replies, dietary and accessibility notes, photos and files. Confirmed twice, because it is the privacy promise.
